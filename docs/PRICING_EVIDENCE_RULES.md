# iPhone Cashify evidence: pricing rules

Generated by `scripts/pricing-evidence/render_rules_report.py`. Each rule states what was measured, where it applies, how many quotes support it, and whether it is **measured** (shown directly by quotes of the model it is applied to), **inferred** (carried from sibling models / the shared template without confirmation on this model) or **unsupported** (the engine does not price it). Accuracy figures are in `PRICING_EVIDENCE_VALIDATION.md`.

## 1. The three prices that must never be mixed

| price | what it is | relation |
|---|---|---|
| **base price** | Cashify's 'Get Upto' for a flawless phone; the engine's *Cashify-equivalent* is on this basis | selling price + ₹20 (2,238 of 2,238 quotes that record both) |
| **headline selling price** | the figure Cashify shows as the quote | base price - ₹20 IMEI validation fee |
| **payout after fees** | what the customer is paid | selling price - processing fee: ₹49 up to ₹2,880; ₹69 for ₹4,090-4,190; ₹99 from ₹5,760 (the ranges in between were never observed) |

Fhoneify's own deduction is a flat ₹99 and it charges no IMEI fee, so for the same Cashify-equivalent a Fhoneify customer is structurally about ₹20 ahead before any uplift. The payout ceiling (`lib/pricing/payoutCeiling.ts`) counts that as part of the extra.

## 2. How a quote is built

1. **Reference** R = Cashify's Get Upto for the exact variant (the existing `ReferencePrice` path, unchanged).
2. **Evidence model** (`lib/pricing/appleCashifyModel`) computes the Cashify-equivalent: R minus the age effect, minus the condition deductions, minus the box/eSIM deductions, never below the model's floor, rounded to ₹10. Every rupee appears in a breakdown labelled `measured`, `inferred` or `rule`.
3. **Existing uplift** `applyCompetitorUplift` (8% up to ₹20,000 of Get Upto, 6% up to ₹50,000, else 4%; extra capped at ₹2,000) is applied **once**, unchanged.
4. **Payout ceiling**: the customer payout - after the ₹99 fee and including the first-time coupon - may not exceed the Cashify-equivalent *payout* by more than min(8%, ₹2,000). It only ever lowers a quote or trims the coupon bonus; it never raises anything.

Calibration data (`calibration.json`, `scope.json`, `template.json`) is separate from the logic (`model.ts`). A model is used only if it is enabled in `scope.json` (held-out validation) **and** the storage variant is one that was measured; every other iPhone, and any answer the model cannot read, keeps the existing engine untouched.

## 3. Rules

### R1. Cashify skips its age question, and prices the phone as over 11 months old, for exactly six answers

Touch screen not working, screen not original, cracked/broken glass, cracked side/back panel, missing panel, bent/curved panel. It does **not** do so for spots, lines, discoloration, scratches, dents, or a loose screen. ### R2. The age / out-of-warranty effect is applied once, and 'Warranty = No', 'Bill = No' and the R1 answers are the same state

In 55 variant-days where warranty No, bill No and age 'above 11 months' were all quoted, 55 gave one identical price. Combining a trigger answer with any age/warranty answer never adds a second age deduction (pairs T0556-T0563: panel/body/spots + cracked glass). The deduction is `a + b x reference` per age step (3-6, 6-11, over 11 months), fitted per model; for models whose variants do not follow one line (iPhone 15, 15 Plus, 16e) the measured percentage per variant is stored and preferred. Status: **measured** per model/variant; for a storage variant that was not measured it is **inferred** from the model's line.

### R3. Screen faults share one deduction: the largest wins

Cracked glass / chipped glass / scratches (S), spots (D01-D03), lines (D04-D05), discoloration (D06-D07), touch failure (Q02) and non-original screen (Q03) are priced as one group: the largest of them applies, plus the single age effect when cracked glass, touch or non-original forces it. Evidence: all 28 Screen x Display pairs and the Screen x Panel pairs on the iPhone 18 Pro Max 2 TB are explained exactly by this rule, and the multi-fault checklist shapes M08, M11, M12 on 15 other models.

The source's claim that 36 differ is therefore **confirmed and explained, not just counted**. The pair plan never combined a functional fault with a physical one, so additivity *across* those groups is only supported by the multi-fault checklist shapes (e.g. M09), not pair by pair.

### R4. Body damage and panel damage share one deduction (largest wins); under cracked glass most of the body deduction disappears

Body scratches/dents (C01-C04) and panel/frame damage (P01-P04) compete (checklist M09: body + loose screen = the loose-screen deduction). When cracked glass has already forced the aged state, body damage adds only 37% (more than 2 scratches, 1-2 dents), 48% (major dents) or 0% (1-2 scratches) of its normal deduction, and a loose screen deducts the same as a cracked panel. Measured on the iPhone 18 Pro Max 2 TB only (pairs T0556-T0559, T0560-T0563); the percentages are **inferred** for every other model. Status: panel/body group **measured**; the under-glass reduction **measured once, inferred elsewhere**.

### R5. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth overlap; the other functional faults add up

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth together deduct the same as either alone (≈30-31% of price). Cameras, speaker/receiver/microphone/charging port, buttons, vibration, proximity and Face ID add up (63 of 63 observed functional pairs on the 18 Pro Max are additive; checklist shapes M02, M04, M05, M07 on 15 models). Battery in service vs battery health 80-85% is treated as one group (largest wins): **inferred** (no pair measured it).

### R7. A floor applies only once the over-11-months state is forced

Heavily damaged phones stop losing value at a plateau: several different shapes quote the same share of the price. The plateau is iPhone 15 31.1% of the aged price, iPhone 15 Plus 26.3% of the reference, iPhone 15 Pro 26.0% of the reference, iPhone 15 Pro Max 26.0% of the reference, iPhone 16 17.7% of the reference, iPhone 16 Plus 17.7% of the reference and 10 more. It never appears in the younger states, which is why a phone with seventeen simultaneous functional faults is *not* floored (see R10; the engine sends those to inspection). The floor basis (reference vs aged price) is chosen per model by which explains that model's plateau. Status: **measured** where listed, otherwise no floor is applied (the safe direction).

### R8. Box, charger, eSIM

Missing box: a fixed amount (₹94-₹2,280 across the 17 measured iPhone 15-18 models; about ₹1,200 on the 14) that is smaller once the aged state applies (₹1,500 against ₹2,250 on the iPhone 18 Pro Max). Missing charger: no deduction wherever the question was offered (the 18 series; most flows do not offer it). Dual eSIM: about 5% of the price on the 9 models whose flow asks it (iPhone 14 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, iPhone 16 Pro, iPhone 16 Pro Max, iPhone 17, iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone 17 Pro Max, iPhone Air). Status: box **measured**; charger **measured for the 18 series only**.

### R9. Cannot make or receive calls

On its own it quotes a fixed ₹1,200 scrap (₹1,180 selling price) - measured on the iPhone 18 Pro Max only (**inferred** for every other model). The only combination measured with it (calls + touch + screen + Wi-Fi + Face ID + Bluetooth) was declined by Cashify in every model that was tried; other combinations were never measured, so the engine sends *any* combination with another fault to inspection rather than guessing. Never stored as ₹0.

### R10. Cashify declines some conditions ('device seems to have very little value')

51 quotes were declined across 2 distinct conditions (`Q01,Q02,Q03,F05,F08,F14` x 38; `all 17 functional faults (F01-F17)` x 13). The all-faults phone that Cashify did quote ranged from 0.6% to 29.7% of the price (35 quotes), so no rule can be read from it. The engine routes both conditions to manual inspection instead of inventing a price.

## 5. Not supported (falls back, never guessed)

- Any iPhone model or storage variant without its own quotes (iPhone 11, 12, 13 and earlier, 14 Plus, 14 Pro Max, SE, XR, XS, and storage variants that were not measured) - existing engine, unchanged.
- Models that did not meet the held-out thresholds - existing engine (see the scope table in `PRICING_EVIDENCE_VALIDATION.md`).
- Conditions Cashify declines, and phones with ten or more functional faults - manual inspection (422 MANUAL_INSPECTION_REQUIRED), not a price.
- Answers the model cannot map (unknown hardware ids such as hinge, S-Pen) - existing engine.
- Larger combinations: only ~2 measured shapes have more than 5 faults. They are priced by the same group rules, which hold on those shapes, but they are the least validated region.
